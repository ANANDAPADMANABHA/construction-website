Most construction problems trace back to something that wasn't checked before work
began. A little diligence up front saves a lot of cost and stress later. Here's a
practical pre-construction checklist for building in Kerala.

## 1. Plot and title

- Confirm clear ownership and title documents
- Verify the plot's exact dimensions and boundaries
- Check access for construction vehicles and materials

## 2. Approvals and building rules

- Identify the correct authority (Panchayat, Municipality or Corporation)
- Confirm setbacks, coverage and height rules for your plot
- Plan the [building permit](/services/building-permit) early — before finalising design

## 3. Soil and site conditions

- Assess soil type and bearing capacity (affects foundation design)
- Check the water table and drainage
- Note slope and whether retaining structures are needed

## 4. Design finalised on paper

- Settle the [2D floor plan](/services/2d-house-plans) — layout, light, ventilation, flow
- Finalise the [3D elevation](/services/3d-elevation-design) before construction
- Lock down specifications so the estimate is accurate

## 5. Budget and contingency

- Build the budget from an **itemised estimate**, not a rough rate
- Keep a sensible contingency for unforeseen conditions
- Understand the payment schedule and what each stage covers
- See our [budget planning guide](/blog/how-to-plan-house-construction-budget)

## 6. The builder and the agreement

- Choose a builder on transparency and accountability, not just price
  ([how to choose](/blog/how-to-choose-construction-company-trivandrum))
- Get a **written agreement** with specifications and a stage-wise payment schedule
- Confirm your single point of contact and how progress is reported

## 7. Services planned, not improvised

- Ensure [electrical and plumbing (MEP)](/services/mep-drawings) are designed, not
  decided ad hoc on site
- Plan waterproofing for roof, bathrooms and sunken areas from the start

## The takeaway

The homes that build smoothly are the ones where these checks were done **before**
breaking ground. Rushing to start is the most expensive way to save time.
