If you're planning to build a home in Thiruvananthapuram, the first question is
almost always about cost. It's also the question with the least honest answer
online — because the true cost depends on decisions you haven't made yet.

Rather than quote a number that will be wrong for your project, here's what
actually determines the cost of building a house in Trivandrum, so you can budget
realistically.

## The main factors that determine cost

### 1. Built-up area
Cost scales with total built-up area across all floors. A larger home costs more
in absolute terms, though the rate per square foot can improve slightly at scale.

### 2. Finish level
This is the biggest swing factor. The same structure can be finished as a simple,
durable home or a premium one — and the difference in flooring, fittings,
joinery, painting and bathrooms can change the total cost dramatically. Two
"1,800 sq.ft houses" can differ by a wide margin purely on finishes.

### 3. Number of floors and structural design
Extra floors, larger spans, cantilevers and complex structures need more steel
and concrete. A straightforward two-storey home is more economical per square
foot than a heavily articulated design.

### 4. Site conditions
Soil type, slope, water table, access for materials and the need for retaining
structures all affect foundation and logistics costs. A tight urban plot and an
open suburban plot are not the same to build on.

### 5. Design complexity
Simple, efficient plans build economically. Unusual shapes, large glazing, double
heights and elaborate elevations add cost — sometimes worth it, but worth knowing
in advance.

### 6. Approvals and services
[Building permit](/services/building-permit) approvals, and properly designed
[MEP services](/services/mep-drawings), are part of the real cost of a home even
though they're sometimes left out of headline quotes.

## Why per-square-foot rates mislead

A single per-sq.ft rate bundles all of the above into one number and then hides
them. That's why two quotes for the "same" house can differ so much — they're
assuming different finishes and inclusions. When comparing builders, compare
**what's included**, not just the rate.
