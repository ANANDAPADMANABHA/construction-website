Every building in Kerala needs approval from the local authority before
construction begins. The process is manageable, but it trips people up when they
start late or submit incomplete drawings. Here's a plain-language overview.

*This is general guidance. Rules change and vary by locality — always confirm the
current requirements for your specific plot.*

## Step 1: Identify the right authority

Your plot falls under one of three local bodies depending on location:

- **Grama Panchayat** — rural and semi-urban areas
- **Municipality** — town areas
- **Corporation** — city areas (such as Thiruvananthapuram Corporation)

Each has its own rules and process. Confirming the correct authority is the first
step, because it determines everything that follows.

## Step 2: Understand the applicable building rules

Approvals are granted against building rules covering things like:

- **Setbacks** — the required open space around the building
- **Coverage and FAR** — how much of the plot you can build on
- **Height** limits
- **Use** — residential, commercial, etc.

Your design has to respect these from the start. Designing first and checking
rules later is how expensive redesigns happen.

## Step 3: Prepare permit (sanction) drawings

The application requires a specific set of drawings prepared to the authority's
standards — site plan, floor plans, sections and elevation, with the required
details and calculations. Drawings that don't meet the rules get returned, adding
weeks.

## Step 4: Assemble documents

Applications typically need ownership and tax documents, plot details and other
supporting paperwork. Incomplete documentation is one of the most common causes
of delay.

## Step 5: Submit, follow up, and get the sanction

Once submitted, the application moves through the authority's review. Following up
and responding to queries promptly keeps it moving. When approved, you receive the
sanction — and only then should construction begin.

## Where projects get delayed

- Starting the permit process too late in the timeline
- Drawings that don't meet local rules
- Missing or incorrect documents
- Designing without checking setbacks and coverage first
