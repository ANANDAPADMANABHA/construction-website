The builder you choose matters more than almost any other decision in your
project — more than the exact plot, more than the finishes. A good team delivers a
home you enjoy for decades; the wrong one delivers stress, overruns and problems
that surface years later. Here's how to choose well.

## 1. Ask for an itemised estimate, not just a rate

Any serious builder can give you a **stage-wise, itemised estimate** tied to your
plan. If all you get is a single per-square-foot number, you can't see what's
included — and you can't meaningfully compare two builders. Insist on the detail.

## 2. Check what's included — and what isn't

Compare quotes on **inclusions**, not headline price. Common exclusions that
inflate a "cheap" quote later: approvals, compound walls, gates, quality fittings,
waterproofing and proper MEP. A higher quote that includes these may be cheaper in
reality.

## 3. Look for a written agreement

A written agreement with **specifications and a payment schedule tied to completed
work** protects both sides. Paying ahead of progress is a common source of
disputes. The schedule should follow the build, not lead it.

## 4. Verify accountability

Who is your single point of contact? Is design, structure and finishing under one
team, or are you coordinating separate vendors? Single-team accountability means
no one can shift blame when something needs fixing.

## 5. See real work and talk to real clients

Ask to see genuine completed projects and, where possible, speak to past clients.
Photographs of real work — not stock images — tell you a lot.

## 6. Ask about supervision and quality

How is quality supervised at critical stages — foundation, concrete,
waterproofing? These are what determine how a home performs over decades, and
they're invisible once finished.

## 7. Watch for the warning signs

- A quote far below others, with no breakdown
- Pressure to pay large amounts upfront
- Reluctance to put specifications in writing
- Vague answers about approvals and supervision
- Only stock photos, no real projects

## The bottom line

Choose on **transparency and accountability**, not just price. The cheapest quote
is rarely the cheapest home once it's built.
